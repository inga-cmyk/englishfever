## Practice recording → b1-unit-08-listening-practice.mp3

Assistant: Good afternoon. Can I help you?
Customer: Yes, please. I'm looking for a winter jacket.
Assistant: Certainly. What size are you?
Customer: Medium, I think.
Assistant: Here is a nice one. It's warm and comfortable, and it's on sale.
Customer: How much is it?
Assistant: It was a hundred and twenty euros, but now it's ninety. That's a twenty-five percent discount.
Customer: Hmm. That's a bit expensive. Do you have anything cheaper?
Assistant: This blue one is seventy euros. It's not as warm, but it's lighter.
Customer: Can I try it on?
Assistant: Of course. The fitting rooms are over there.
Customer: ... It fits well. I'll take it. Can I pay by card?
Assistant: Yes, you can. Here is your receipt. If you want to return it, you have to do it within fourteen days.
Customer: Great, thank you.

## Graded quiz recording → b1-unit-08-listening-quiz.mp3

Presenter: Welcome to the money podcast. Today we have three simple tips for saving money.
First, make a budget. Write down how much money you earn and how much you spend. Many people are surprised when they see how much they spend on coffee, snacks and fast food. One listener told me that she spent a hundred euros a month on takeaway coffee! She said that she had never realised it.
Second, set a goal. If you know what you are saving for, it's easier to save. For example, if you want to travel, open a separate savings account and put a small amount into it every month. Even ten euros makes a difference.
Third, wait before you buy. When you see something you want, wait for 48 hours. If you still want it after two days, buy it. In most cases, you won't want it any more.
Of course, there are many other ways to save, such as buying second-hand clothes or comparing prices online. But these three tips are a good start. And remember: it's not how much you earn, it's how much you keep.
Thank you for listening, and see you next week.
