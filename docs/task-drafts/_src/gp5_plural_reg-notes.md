# Batch 5, point `plural_reg` (A2.1): notes of the writer

Topic: Irregular plurals (`irregular_plurals`), level A2.1. Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, Appendix 6 (spelling): *-es* after *-s, -sh, -ch, -x* (*buses, dishes, watches, boxes*); consonant + *-y* → *-ies* (*city → cities*); vowel + *-y* → *-ys* (*day → days, key → keys*). Unit 66/68 for singular and plural nouns in general. No *-o* nouns (*potatoes / photos*) and no *-f / -fe* nouns: they are not on this point (the *-ves* words belong to the irregular point and are already in the bank at A2.2).

The point had 1 counted task (a typed gap, *use*: *Riga and Tallinn are beautiful ___. (city)*) and no *check* task, so most new tasks are choices and word banks.

## Mix (9) and why
4 choice, 2 word bank, 2 typed gap, 1 whole sentence. No word order: the brief says a plural is a spelling of one word, and putting words in order would not need it; the two word-order places went to one more choice and kept the typed gaps at 2. Result: 6 *check* (4 choice + 2 bank), 3 *use* (2 gaps + 1 sentence), with the existing gap 4 *use*.
Spellings covered: *-ies* (countries, parties, libraries), vowel + *-y* (days, keys), *-es* (watches, boxes, classes, buses, dishes), plain *-s* (bags, brothers). Settings: travel, work, shopping, mixed, moving house, lifestyle, office, learning, travel (no setting twice in a row).

## What the learner must know, task by task
1. *We visited three ___ on our trip.* → *countries*: consonant + *-y* → *-ies*. *countrys* (only *-s*) and *countryes* (*-es* added to the *y*) are the two spellings learners really write; *country* after *three* is the "no plural" mistake (*two bus*).
2. *I work late two ___ a week.* → *days*: vowel + *-y* only adds *-s*. *daies* over-applies the *-ies* rule, *dayes* the *-es* rule (both listed as typical on the map); *day* is the missing plural.
3. Which correct: *We bought two watches and three bags.* Each wrong sentence has one mistake: *watchs* (no *-es* after *-ch*), *bages* (*-es* where only *-s*), *two watch* (no plural). The learner must know both rules at once.
4. Has a mistake: *Our town has two new librarys.* (*libraries*). The three correct sentences are about three different things (a trip, housework, family) and contain the other spellings correctly (*days*, *dishes*, *brothers*), so a learner who over-applies a rule (*dayes*, *dishs* look wrong to him) may pick the wrong one.
5. Bank *We need two big ___ for the books.* → *boxes* | *boxs* (common mistake) | *box* (no plural). Only *boxes* gives a correct sentence.
6. Bank *There are lots of ___ in December.* → *parties* | *partys* | *party*. *lots of party* is wrong (countable noun), *partys* is the learner's spelling.
7. Typed *I've got three ___ for the office. (key)* → *keys* only. The trap is *keies*; *have got* is A2.1.
8. Typed *I have two English ___ a week. (class)* → *classes* only. The cue fixes the word (*lessons* would also fit without it).
9. *Start with *Two*: The bus is late.* → *Two buses are late.* The learner must spell *buses* and make *is* plural. *Two busses are late.* is accepted too: *busses* is a rare variant listed in American dictionaries (Murphy and British usage: *buses*); it is not taught.
