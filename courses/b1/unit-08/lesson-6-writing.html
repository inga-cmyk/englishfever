Unit 8 · Writing: informal and formal emails

Goal: write informal and formal emails about shopping and money, using all the B1 grammar you have studied and linking words.

1. Formal or informal?

We choose our words for the reader. We write informal emails to friends and family. We write formal emails to a boss, a teacher, a company or someone we do not know.

Informal (friends)Formal (work, school, companies)
Hi Anna, / Dear Anna,Dear Mr Brown, / Dear Ms Smith, / Dear Sir or Madam,
Thanks for your email. / Great to hear from you!Thank you for your email. / I am writing to ask about ...
Can you ...? / Could you ...?Could you please ...? / I would be grateful if you could ...
I'm sorry, but I can't ...Unfortunately, I am unable to ...
Write soon! / See you! / Take care,I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your help.
Love, / Best wishes, / Bye for now,Yours sincerely, (name known) / Yours faithfully, (name not known)

Tips: In informal emails you can use short forms (I'm, can't), phrasal verbs and exclamation marks. In formal emails use full forms (I am, cannot), polite words (please, would, could) and no slang.

2. A good email has four parts

  1. Greeting – Dear ... / Hi ...
  2. Reason – why you are writing (1 sentence)
  3. Main part – the details, one idea in each paragraph
  4. Ending – a closing sentence and a sign-off

3. Coherence and cohesion

Coherence means your ideas are in a clear order and the reader can follow them. Use paragraphs. Put one idea in each paragraph.

Cohesion means the sentences are joined with small words. Use linking words:

UseLinking words
addingand, also, too, as well as, in addition, moreover (formal)
contrastbut, however, although, on the other hand, in spite of
reason / resultbecause, so, as, therefore, that is why
orderfirst, then, after that, finally, at the end
examplefor example, for instance, such as

Also use reference words so you do not repeat nouns: My brother has a new job. He likes it very much. This is good news.

4. Unit 8 examples

Informal email

Task: Your friend borrowed money and wants to know when you need it back. Also tell them about something you bought last week.

Hi Karlis,

Thanks for your message! Don't worry about the money – you can give it back next month. I know you're saving for a new bike, aren't you?

By the way, I bought some new trainers last week. They were on sale, so they only cost 40 euros instead of 80. What a bargain! However, they were a bit too big, so I had to exchange them. Next time I will try them on first!

Let me know if you want to meet at the weekend. If it doesn't rain, we could go for a walk.

Best wishes,
Mara

Formal email

Task: You bought a product online but it arrived damaged. Write to the company to complain and ask for a refund.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to complain about a coffee machine which I ordered from your website on 12 May (order number 7421). When the parcel arrived, I noticed that the machine was broken. Unfortunately, it cannot be used.

I paid 120 euros for it, so I would be grateful if you could send me a full refund. I have attached my receipt and photographs of the damage. If I do not receive a reply within ten days, I will contact the consumer protection office.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
Mara Ozola

Notice: These emails use many tenses: past simple (bought, cost), present perfect, first conditional (If it doesn't rain, ...), quantity words (too big, enough), a question tag (aren't you?) and the passive (cannot be used). Linking words: however, by the way, so, unfortunately, if.
