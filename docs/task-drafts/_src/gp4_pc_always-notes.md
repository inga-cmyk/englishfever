# Batch 4, point `pc_always` (B1.2): notes of the writer

Topic: Present continuous (`present_continuous`). Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, unit 3 (*I always do* and *I'm always doing*: *I'm always losing things* = it happens too often and the speaker is annoyed). The past form (*was always complaining*) is not taught in a Murphy unit of its own; it is the same pattern with *was / were* (the map's `x_pastc_always` notes the same gap in AGU).

Principle: *He always loses his keys* is correct English, only neutral. So the simple form is never a wrong option. The choices test the form (*is always losing*, not *always is losing / is always lose / always losing*), the position of *always*, the agreement and the past. Meaning (which sentence complains) is tested only in learning tasks. Typed tasks accept the simple form where it is correct; only *Complain with *always* / *constantly*:* asks for the continuous.

## Mix (25, as in the brief)
7 choice (one of them a learning task), 5 typed gap, 3 whole sentence, 5 word order, 4 word bank, 1 matching (a learning task).

## What the learner must know, task by task
Choice
1. *My flatmate ___ my phone charger without asking, and it drives me mad.*: *is always borrowing*. The wrong options are the three form mistakes of this point: *always is*, a base verb after *is always*, no *is*. *always borrows* is not offered (it is correct).
2. *Which sentence is correct?* *My neighbours are always parking …*: position. Against *always are parking*, *are always park*, *are parking always*.
3. *When we worked together, Lisa ___ about the air conditioning.*: *was always complaining*. Against the position mistake, the base verb and the present (*is always complaining* does not fit a finished past). *always complained* is not offered (correct).
4. NOT correct: *My dad is always lose his glasses …*. The three correct ones are about three things (phone at dinner, money in the past, a birthday with the neutral simple form): it shows that *always forgets* is correct.
5. *My teenage sons ___ always leaving wet towels …*: *are*. Against *do / have* (the wrong helping verb with -ing) and *is* (agreement).
6. *My manager is constantly ___ us emails …*: *sending*. Against *send / sends / sent* after *is constantly*.
7. Learning task: *Which sentence is a complaint?* *My partner is always leaving the lights on.* The others are a fact now, a routine and a plan; no simple *always* sentence is among them.

Typed gap
8. *Complain with *always*: My flatmates ___ (leave) …*: *are always leaving | 're always leaving*. The instruction asks for the complaint, so *always leave* is not accepted (the lead's rule). *never clean up* keeps it in the present.
9. *Complain with *always*: When we shared an office, Mark ___ (hum) …*: *was always humming*; also accepted *would always hum / 'd always hum* (correct English for an annoying past habit). **The feedback shown is about *was always*; an answer note for *would* is needed** (lib.py writes the answer-notes column empty), e.g. `would always hum :: *would always* + verb is also correct for a past habit; *was always humming* is the complaint form of this topic. :: *would always* + darbības vārds arī ir pareizi pagātnes ieradumam; *was always humming* ir šīs tēmas īgnuma forma.`
10. *We need a new printer: ours ___ always ___ in the middle of big jobs. (jam)*: *is / jamming*. The two gaps around *always* need *be* + -ing; *We need* fixes the present (*was jamming* was possible without it). *does / jam* is not accepted: *does always jam* is not natural.
11. *My old car ___ constantly ___ down, so in the end I sold it. (break)*: *was / breaking*; *sold* fixes the past.
12. *Use the words in brackets: My brother ___ (always / forget) my birthday …*: both *always forgets* and *is always forgetting* (and *'s always forgetting*) are accepted; the task checks the position of *always*, and the explanation says both are right.

Whole sentence
13. *Complain with *constantly*: My boss calls me during my lunch break.*: *My boss is constantly calling me …* (also *boss's*).
14. *Complain with *always*: When we were students, my flatmate borrowed my notes.*: *was always borrowing*, both clause orders; *would always borrow* accepted too (same note as task 9 needed).
15. *Make a question with *Why*: You're always interrupting me in meetings.*: *Why are you always interrupting me (in meetings)?* and *Why do you always interrupt me (in meetings)?* (both correct; the version without *in meetings* is accepted as bad_examples asks).

Word order (subjects with a contraction, so the cards do not also make a yes/no question)
16. *my sister's always borrowing my clothes without asking*.
17. *it's always raining when i want to go cycling* (also the *when*-part first).
18. Question: *why are you always checking your phone at dinner*. **Written with *Put the words in order to make a question.*, but `plain()` in lib.py rewrites it to *Put the words in order.*** The lead said it keeps the question instruction: this needs the change in `plain()` (no other order is possible from these cards, so either instruction is safe).
19. *he's forever telling us how busy he is* (8 words; *at work* was dropped because it can also go after *us* or in front).
20. *you're always making plans for the weekend without telling me*.

Word bank (wrong chips are real forms that do not fit; no chip that agrees wrongly with the subject)
21. *My colleague ___ ___ ___ my yoghurt …*: *is always taking*; *takes / does* cannot make a correct three-word answer (*always takes* fills only two gaps).
22. *When I was a student, my landlord ___ always ___ round …*: *was dropping*; *is* clashes with *When I was a student*; *drop* after *was always* is the form mistake.
23. *My parents ___ always ___ me when I'm going to find a better job.*: *are asking*; *have* + *asking* and *are* + *ask* are wrong; *asked* is not a chip, so *have always asked* cannot be built.
24. *My phone ___ constantly ___ me to update my apps …*: *is reminding*; *has / reminds* do not fit.

Matching (learning task)
25. Verb in the beginning, its object in the ending (leaving + the front door unlocked, changing + the meeting times, borrowing + my phone charger, barking + at the postman). Every swap was read: none makes sense.

## Card line needed (the card has only *always* in the present)
- EN (watch): "*always / constantly / forever* + continuous shows annoyance: *He's always [[losing]] his keys! She was always [[complaining]].*"
- LV (watch): "*always / constantly / forever* + Continuous izsaka īgnumu: *He's always [[losing]] his keys! She was always [[complaining]].*"
This replaces the present *always* line; tasks 3, 6, 9, 11, 13, 14, 19, 22, 24, 25 need *constantly / forever* or the past.

## Unsure
- Tasks 8, 13, 14 (*Complain with …*) accept only the continuous, as the lead decided; a reader may argue that *always leave* is also a complaint. If the judge disagrees, the fix is to accept the simple form with a note.
- Tasks 9 and 14 accept *would always*: correct English, but not on this card; the answer note above is needed so the learner does not see the *was always* explanation for it.
