Unit 4 · Grammar: Modal verbs: must, have to, should, might, can't

Goal: talk about rules, advice, obligation, possibility and certainty.

1. Modal verbs are special

After a modal verb we use the base verb (no to, no -s): She must wear a uniform. (not She must wears)

MeaningVerbExample
strong obligation (rules)must / have toYou must show your ID. Students have to be on time.
no obligationdon't have toYou don't have to wear a tie. (It is not necessary.)
it is forbiddenmustn'tYou mustn't use your phone in the exam.
adviceshould / shouldn'tYou should sleep more. You shouldn't work so late.
permission and abilitycan / could / mayCan I leave early? Could you help me?

Be careful: mustn't and don't have to are very different! You mustn't park here = it is not allowed. You don't have to park here = you can park somewhere else.

2. Past forms

must has no past form. Use had to: Yesterday I had to work late. For advice about the past use should have + past participle: You should have studied more. (You didn't, and it was a mistake.)

3. Possibility and certainty (deduction)

How sure?VerbExample
almost sure it's truemust beHe works 12 hours a day. He must be tired.
maybemight / may / couldShe might get the job. I could be late.
almost sure it's not truecan't beThat can't be his car. He doesn't have one.
