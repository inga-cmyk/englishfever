# Batch 4, point `no_none` (B1.1): notes of the writer

Topic: Quantity (`quantity`). Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, unit 86 (*no / none / any*; *nothing, nobody* etc.): *no* + noun = *not a / not any* + noun (*There's no sugar* = *There isn't any sugar*); *none* without a noun (*How much money have you got? None.*) and *none of* + *the / this / my / them*; *no* + noun and *none of* can start a sentence (*No cars are allowed…*, *None of the students…*); after *none of* + plural Murphy allows a singular or a plural verb, so no task makes the learner choose the verb (past verbs, *could*, *came out*, *knew* are used instead); only one negative in the sentence.

## Mix (25, as in the brief)
7 choice, 5 typed gap, 3 whole sentence, 5 word order (1 question), 4 word bank, 1 matching (a learning task).

## What the learner must know, task by task
Choice
1. *…there was ___ hot water in our room.*: a noun follows and *was* is positive, so *no*. Wrong: *none* (the *None people came* mistake), *any* in a positive sentence, *none of* without *the*. *not any* is NOT an option: *there was not any hot water* is correct English.
2. *…there was ___ left.*: no noun after the gap, so *none*. Wrong: *no* (needs a noun), *any*, *none of*. *nothing* and *not any* were left out on purpose: both are correct here.
3. Which correct: *There aren't any shops in our village…* against *aren't no shops* (double negative), *are any shops*, *are none shops*.
4. Which correct: *None of my friends could come…* against *No of*, *None my friends*, *None … couldn't* (double negative). *could* avoids the singular / plural verb question.
5. NOT correct: *I don't have nothing to wear…* (the double negative with *nothing*: the neighbouring point appears only as the answer of a NOT-correct task, as the lead allowed). The other three are correct and about three different things: *There's no time* (no + noun), *None of the shops opened* (none of, past verb), *I haven't got any cash* (not any).
6. *'How many tickets are left…?' '___. They're all sold out.'*: a short answer without a noun is *None*. Wrong: *No* (needs a noun / is a yes-no answer), *Any*, *No one* (people). The two-line exchange is needed: the answer is a reply. *Nothing* was left out (some speakers use it).
7. *…five language schools…, but ___ them had a free place.*: before *them* use *none of*. Wrong: *no*, *none* (without *of*), *any of* (positive verb).

Typed gap (the instruction names the two words where several words would otherwise fit)
8. *Write *no* or *any*: My new phone has ___ headphone jack…* → *no* (positive verb).
9. *Write *no* or *any*: Our teacher didn't give us ___ homework…* → *any* (negative verb; *no* = double negative).
10. *Write *no* or *none*: …but there was ___ left.* → *none* (no noun). Without the two words, *nothing* and *little* fit too; that is why they are named.
11. *Write *no* or *none*: ___ of the photos I took at the concert came out well. It was too dark.* → *None* (before *of*).
12. *Complete with one word: There ___ any direct flights, so we had to change planes in Frankfurt.* → *weren't | aren't* (a general fact with *aren't* is correct with the past result; *were not / are not* by the checker's n't rule). *wasn't* is not accepted (plural).

Whole sentence
13. *Say it another way with *no*: There aren't any vegetarian dishes on the menu.* → *There are no vegetarian dishes on the menu.* (also with *On the menu* first). *There's no vegetarian dishes* is not accepted.
14. *Say it another way with *any*: I've got no plans for the weekend.* → *I haven't got / I don't have / I've not got / I haven't any plans for the weekend.*
15. *Correct the mistake: We didn't get no answer from the bank…* → *didn't get any answer*, *got no answer* or *didn't get an answer* (all three corrections are accepted; the grader also takes the corrected words alone).

Word order
16. *i didn't have any time to revise for the test*
17. *none of my colleagues knew that the meeting was cancelled*
18. *Put the words in order to make a question.* *why are there no taxis at the station* (no statement is possible with these cards). First draft was *…no buses to the airport on sundays*: changed because *on Sundays to the airport* is a second order people say.
19. *no cars are allowed in the old town at weekends*, also *at weekends no cars…* and *in the old town no cars are allowed at weekends*.
20. *we had no idea that they were getting married*

Word bank (the wrong chips are the mistakes of this point, every one a real word: double negative, *none* + noun, *No of*, *any* in a positive sentence)
21. *Sorry, there ___ ___ seats left on the morning train.* [are, no, aren't, none] → *are no*; *aren't no*, *are none*, *aren't none* are all wrong. There is no *any* chip, so *aren't any* cannot arise.
22. *___ ___ the students passed the first test…* [None, of, No, Any] → *None of*; *No of*, *Any of* are wrong.
23. *Our phones had ___ signal in the mountains…* [no, any, none] → *no*.
24. *We haven't got ___ coffee left…* [any, no, none] → *any*.

Matching (learning)
25. *there were no* + *rooms for us.* / *none of* + *them replied.* / *I don't have any* + *cash on me.* / *there was* + *none left in the pot.* Each beginning takes only its own ending: *there were no them*, *none of rooms*, *there were no cash*, *there was rooms*, *don't have any none* are all wrong; *I don't have any rooms for us* is grammatical but makes no sense after *I can't pay for the taxi*.

## Card lines needed (Quantity card)
The card has only *no = not any* (Watch out). The tasks also use *none* and the one-negative rule. Proposed lines:
- form (EN): *no* + noun = *not any* + noun: *There's no milk.* = *There isn't any milk.* *none* has no noun after it: *How many came? None.* *none of* + *the / my / them*: *None of my friends came.*
- form (LV): *no* + lietvārds = *not any* + lietvārds: *There's no milk.* = *There isn't any milk.* Aiz *none* lietvārda nav: *How many came? None.* *none of* + *the / my / them*: *None of my friends came.*
- watch (EN): Only one negative: *There isn't any milk* or *There's no milk*, not *There isn't no milk*.
- watch (LV): Tikai viens noliegums: *There isn't any milk* vai *There's no milk*, nevis *There isn't no milk*.

## The Latvian *no* (needs the lead)
In the Latvian explanations, the English *no* is never put in italics: *no* is in `italics.LV_ASCII` (Latvian *no* = from), and `lv_mark` makes an exception only for *no longer / no more*. Result now: *There was* no *bread*. I wrote every Latvian text so that each English *no* is followed by an English word (*no milk, no bread, No of, no one, no tickets*). With this rule in `lv_mark` (tried in a scratch copy of `italics.py`, not in the repository) all 25 Latvian texts come out right:
`if w.lower() == "no" and i + 1 < len(toks) and (toks[i + 1][0].lower() in ("longer", "more") or kind[i + 1] == "E"):`
A general rule could mark a Latvian *no* before an English example word in other files (*atšķiras no *will**), so make it apply to this file only, or check the published explanations after the change.

## Unsure / for the judge
- Task 6: *No one* as a wrong option is weaker than the others (a mix-up of *none* and *no one*), but it is wrong here.
- Task 12: *aren't* is accepted with the past *had to* (a timetable fact). If the judge wants one tense only, the sentence would need *last week*.
- Task 22: *Any* as a chip is a real word but a less frequent mistake than *No of*.
- Task 15: *didn't get any answer* is less usual than *didn't get an answer*; both are correct and accepted.
