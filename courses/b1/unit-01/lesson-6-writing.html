Unit 1 · Writing: informal and formal emails

Goal: write informal and formal emails about free time and routines, using present and past tenses and linking words.

1. Formal or informal?

We choose our words for the reader. We write informal emails to friends and family. We write formal emails to a boss, a teacher, a company or someone we do not know.

Informal (friends)Formal (work, school, companies)
Hi Anna, / Dear Anna,Dear Mr Brown, / Dear Ms Smith, / Dear Sir or Madam,
Thanks for your email. / Great to hear from you!Thank you for your email. / I am writing to ask about ...
Can you ...? / Could you ...?Could you please ...? / I would be grateful if you could ...
I'm sorry, but I can't ...Unfortunately, I am unable to ...
Write soon! / See you! / Take care,I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your help.
Love, / Best wishes, / Bye for now,Yours sincerely, (name known) / Yours faithfully, (name not known)

Tips: In informal emails you can use short forms (I'm, can't), phrasal verbs and exclamation marks. In formal emails use full forms (I am, cannot), polite words (please, would, could) and no slang.

2. A good email has four parts

  1. Greeting – Dear ... / Hi ...
  2. Reason – why you are writing (1 sentence)
  3. Main part – the details, one idea in each paragraph
  4. Ending – a closing sentence and a sign-off

3. Coherence and cohesion

Coherence means your ideas are in a clear order and the reader can follow them. Use paragraphs. Put one idea in each paragraph.

Cohesion means the sentences are joined with small words. Use linking words:

UseLinking words
addingand, also, too, as well as, in addition, moreover (formal)
contrastbut, however, although, on the other hand, in spite of
reason / resultbecause, so, as, therefore, that is why
orderfirst, then, after that, finally, at the end
examplefor example, for instance, such as

Also use reference words so you do not repeat nouns: My brother has a new job. He likes it very much. This is good news.

4. Unit 1 examples

Informal email

Task: Your friend Sam asked what you do in your free time. Write back and invite him to join you.

Hi Sam,

Thanks for your email – great to hear from you! You asked about my free time. Well, I usually go to the gym after work, and at the weekend I hang out with friends. I have also taken up cycling, and I love it!

Last Saturday we cycled thirty kilometres to the lake. It was tiring, but we had a lovely picnic. Why don't you join us next weekend? Let me know if you can come.

Write soon!
Anna

Formal email

Task: You want to join a sports club. Write to the club manager and ask about the classes and the price.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to ask about the classes at your sports club. I live nearby, and I am looking for a new hobby. I have never done yoga before, so I would like to start with a class for beginners.

Could you please tell me when the classes are and how much they cost? In addition, I would be grateful if you could send me information about membership.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
Anna Ozola

Notice: The informal email uses short forms (don't), exclamation marks and Write soon!. The formal email uses I am writing to ..., Could you please ...? and Yours faithfully. Both use present simple (routines), present perfect (experience) and past simple (last Saturday).

Informal: Write to a friend. Tell them about your routine, a new hobby you have started, and what you did last weekend. Invite them to do something together.

Formal: Write to a language school. Ask about evening English classes. Say who you are, what you do in the week, and ask about the price and the start date.
