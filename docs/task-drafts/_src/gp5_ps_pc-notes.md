# Batch 5, point `ps_pc` (A2.1): notes of the writer

Point: *Present simple or continuous: habit or now*. Map level A2.1; it had 3 check + 1 use tasks (all at A2.2 / B1.1), so N = 6 new tasks, all at A2.1 and all counting (no matching, no meaning questions, no learning task).
Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, unit 3 (*I do* and *I am doing*): the present simple for things in general or repeatedly (*usually, never*), the present continuous for something happening now (*Look!, at the moment, right now*).
Topics: five tasks in *Present continuous* (the card has the contrast line *I live in Riga / I'm living with my parents*), the one task whose answer is the habit form (*do*) in *Present simple*. No temporary situation (*this week, these days*) and no plan: those belong to `pc_temp` (A2.2) and the future.

## Mix (6) and why
2 choice (one gap choice, one *Which sentence has a mistake?*), 1 word order, 1 word bank, 1 typed gap (two gaps), 1 whole sentence. This is the brief's 3/2/2/2/1 scaled to six; the existing four tasks are three choices / gaps and one typed gap, so the new ones lean to drag and writing. Both directions are tested: the habit form wins (tasks 1, 3, 5) and the now form wins (tasks 2, 3, 4, 5, 6).

## What the learner must know, task by task
1. mc (Present simple) *What time ___ you usually finish work?* → *do*. *usually* makes it a habit, so *do*; *are* is the believable mistake of this point (*Are you finishing work?* is right for today, so *usually* is what rules it out); *—* (no helping verb) is the common A2 question mistake.
2. mc *Which sentence has a mistake?* → *Look! Our bus comes round the corner.* *Look!* needs *is coming*. The three correct ones are about three things: a habit (*We usually have dinner at seven*), an action now (*My brother is sleeping at the moment*), a question about a regular time (*Does your shop open on Sundays?*). The Latvian note: Latvian *nāk* has one present for both, hence *comes*.
3. od *she usually swims but today she is running*: the learner must attach *usually* to *swims* and *today* to *is running*; the wrong build (*she usually is running but today she swims*) is exactly the point's mistake. Also accepted: *usually she swims …*, *… but she is running today*, *today she is running but she usually swims*, *she is running today but she usually swims*.
4. bank *Can I call you back? I ___ dinner.* → *am having*; chips *have* (the habit form, correct in *I have dinner at seven*, so it competes) and *had* (wrong with *Can I call you back?* now). Cover test: without the first sentence *I have dinner* is correct English, so the situation is needed; it is one short sentence.
5. gap *Look! Dad ___ the dishes. He never ___ that! (wash / do)* → *is washing | 's washing / does*. Both forms in one task: *Look!* = now, *never* = habit, *he* → *does*. The present of the scene (*Look!*) keeps *never* in the present.
6. tx *Rewrite the sentence about this moment. Start with *Right now*: My sister reads the news on her phone.* → *Right now my sister is reading the news on her phone.* (also *sister's*; the comma after *Right now* is handled by the checker). The start does not contain the answer; the learner must change *reads* into *is reading*.

- Task 1: only two wrong options (*are*, *—*); *does* / *is* would be agreement mistakes that need no knowledge of the point, so they were left out.
