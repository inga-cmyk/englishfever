Unit 7 · Writing: informal and formal emails

Goal: write informal and formal emails that report news and give opinions, using reported speech, question tags and linking words.

1. Formal or informal?

We choose our words for the reader. We write informal emails to friends and family. We write formal emails to a boss, a teacher, a company or someone we do not know.

Informal (friends)Formal (work, school, companies)
Hi Anna, / Dear Anna,Dear Mr Brown, / Dear Ms Smith, / Dear Sir or Madam,
Thanks for your email. / Great to hear from you!Thank you for your email. / I am writing to ask about ...
Can you ...? / Could you ...?Could you please ...? / I would be grateful if you could ...
I'm sorry, but I can't ...Unfortunately, I am unable to ...
Write soon! / See you! / Take care,I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your help.
Love, / Best wishes, / Bye for now,Yours sincerely, (name known) / Yours faithfully, (name not known)

Tips: In informal emails you can use short forms (I'm, can't), phrasal verbs and exclamation marks. In formal emails use full forms (I am, cannot), polite words (please, would, could) and no slang.

2. A good email has four parts

  1. Greeting – Dear ... / Hi ...
  2. Reason – why you are writing (1 sentence)
  3. Main part – the details, one idea in each paragraph
  4. Ending – a closing sentence and a sign-off

3. Coherence and cohesion

Coherence means your ideas are in a clear order and the reader can follow them. Use paragraphs. Put one idea in each paragraph.

Cohesion means the sentences are joined with small words. Use linking words:

UseLinking words
addingand, also, too, as well as, in addition, moreover (formal)
contrastbut, however, although, on the other hand, in spite of
reason / resultbecause, so, as, therefore, that is why
orderfirst, then, after that, finally, at the end
examplefor example, for instance, such as

Also use reference words so you do not repeat nouns: My brother has a new job. He likes it very much. This is good news.

4. Unit 7 examples

Informal email

Task: You heard some news about your town. Write to a friend to tell them what happened and what people said.

Hi Janis,

Have you heard the news? The council announced that they were going to build a new park next to the river! The mayor said it would be finished by next summer. You'll love it, won't you? It's close to your flat.

Not everybody is happy, though. My neighbour told me that the park would bring too much noise. However, I think it's a great idea, because it will make our town greener. Besides, they said they would plant two hundred trees.

Do you want to come to the opening? Let me know!
Take care,
Kristine

Formal email

Task: You read an article in a newspaper and you disagree. Write a letter to the editor with your opinion.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing in response to your article, "Free transport for all", which was published on 5 May. The article said that free public transport would reduce pollution. However, I do not believe that this is the case.

Firstly, the minister claimed that thousands of people would stop using their cars, but there was no evidence for this. Secondly, the money to pay for the buses will have to come from taxes. Therefore, I would suggest spending the money on better cycle paths instead.

I hope you will consider publishing my opinion.

Yours faithfully,
Kristine Bērziņa

Notice: Both emails use reported speech (the mayor said it would be finished, the article said that...). The informal email uses a question tag (You'll love it, won't you?). In the formal letter note: I am writing in response to ..., Firstly, Secondly, Therefore, I would suggest.
