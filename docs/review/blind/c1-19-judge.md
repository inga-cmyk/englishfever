## Flagged tasks

**c1-19-002 · i'm wondering whether you'd mind swapping seats with me — KEEP.** Polite present continuous + indirect question +
*mind -ing* together is the C1 card's point (Hewings); one natural order.

**c1-19-003 · I ___ ___ more and more how much I miss the sea. — KEEP.** *was realising* has no past anchor and clashes with
*more and more* + present *miss*; both readers call it a believable distractor, not a second answer.

**c1-19-004 · Your brother ___ ___ you've decided to sell the flat. — KEEP.** Reader 2 confirms the key is unique (*is telling*
needs three gaps).

**c1-19-005 · i'm already regretting my decision to move to the countryside — ACCEPT** `already i'm regretting my decision to
move to the countryside` (fronted *already*, correct and used in speech). The end position was already accepted. Level: the
point is the mental-state verb in the continuous (C1 card); kept.

**c1-19-006 · OK, OK, I ___ ___: I forgot to book the restaurant. — KEEP.** Performative present simple (*I admit it*, not
*I'm admitting it*) is a Hewings C1 point and the map's mistake; *I'm admitting it* is what learners say.

**c1-19-007 · I ___ ___ to retire at sixty, but with prices the way they are, I'll have to keep working. — KEEP.** *have
planned* says the plan still stands, which the second half denies; the readers find no second answer.

**c1-19-010 · During last summer's heatwave, we ___ ___ in the garden every night. — DROP.** Both readers: the natural form is
*slept*, which the two gaps rule out, so the learner only chooses between *were sleeping* and *have slept* (finished time: a B1
decision). The past continuous for a temporary repeated situation is always optional (the past simple is also right), so no
word bank can make it the one answer. The topic keeps six drag tasks.

**c1-19-012 · By the time I found my phone, she ___ ___ ___ me four times. — KEEP.** *already had texted* is the American
position; in British English a mid-position adverb goes after the first auxiliary (Murphy 110, Swan), which is what we teach.
It cannot be accepted in a bank gap anyway (one answer per blank).

**c1-19-013 · i had expected the interview to be much harder than it was — ACCEPT** `the interview was much harder than i had
expected it to be` (both readers; natural, the same *had expected* point), `it was much harder than i had expected the
interview to be` (both readers; correct when *it* refers back), `i had expected it to be much harder than the interview was`
(the same pronoun swap). The explanation now names both endings (*than it was*, *than I had expected it to be*).

**c1-19-014 · It was the first time she ___ ___ on the left. — KEEP.** *It was the first time* + past perfect is on the C1 card;
*has / drove* build real wrong forms.

**c1-19-017 · I don't remember ___ ___ this film before. — KEEP.** *remember seeing* has no chip; *having seen* is correct and
*to see* changes the meaning.

**c1-19-019 · the house is thought to have been built in the 1890s — KEEP.** The question order is already accepted; fronted
*in the 1890s the house is thought …* is not natural (c1-18 decision, reader 2 agrees).

**c1-19-020 · i'm afraid i'd have to disagree with you on that — ACCEPT** `i'm afraid i'd have to disagree on that with you`,
`i'd have to disagree on that with you i'm afraid`, `on that i'm afraid i'd have to disagree with you`, `on that i'd have to
disagree with you i'm afraid`, `i'm afraid on that i'd have to disagree with you` (all correct with hidden commas; the point,
*I'd have to disagree*, is in every one). *I'm afraid* at the end was already accepted.

**c1-19-021 · Please allow extra time … in case the roads ___ ___ busy. — KEEP.** *in case … will* is not standard British
English (Swan); the readers call it a fair distractor.

**c1-19-022 · They ___ ___ out in the garden. — KEEP.** *can* for a guess about one situation now is the C1 card's point
(map: *It can be snowing now*); the wrong chip is a real C1 mistake.

**c1-19-023 · The house itself was closed to visitors, but we ___ ___ into the garden. — CHANGE.** Reader 1 is right: the
sentence describes what visitors were generally allowed to do, and *could* is correct for general permission (Swan), so *could
go* is a second answer. New sentence: one particular occasion, achieved after effort, where *could* is wrong both for
permission (Swan 125) and for a single achievement (Murphy 26).
- Gap: `My name was missing from the passenger list, but after an hour of phone calls, I ___ ___ onto the plane.`
- Correct: `was · allowed`
- Wrong chips: `could · go`
- Tried: *was allowed* ✓; *could go* ✗ (one occasion); *was go*, *could allowed* are not forms.
- EN: Permission on one particular occasion in the past: *was allowed* (onto the plane). *could* is for general permission or
  ability, not for one thing that happened on one occasion: not *I could go onto the plane*.
- LV: Atļauja vienā konkrētā reizē pagātnē: *was allowed* (onto the plane). *could* lieto vispārējai atļaujai vai spējai, nevis
  vienam notikumam vienā reizē: nevis *I could go onto the plane*.
- Point id unchanged (`x_allowed_once`).

**c1-19-024 · she may be clever but she has no common sense — KEEP.** The fronted order the reader lists is already accepted.

**c1-19-026 · they might have been delayed by the snow — KEEP.** The question order is already accepted.

**c1-19-027 · The coach advised that the captain ___ ___ to journalists. — KEEP.** *should not speak* has no chip; the bare
subjunctive is correct in British English too (Hewings), reader 1 agrees.

**c1-19-029 · the committee insisted that the decision be announced to all staff — ACCEPT** (house rule: people that can swap
are accepted) `all staff insisted that the decision be announced to the committee`, `the committee insisted to all staff that
the decision be announced`, `all staff insisted to the committee that the decision be announced`, `the staff insisted that the
committee decision be announced to all`, `the committee insisted that the staff decision be announced to all`. All keep
*insisted that … be announced*. The readers' *to all the staff* needs a third *the* (only two cards) and *the staff … to all
committee* is not English.

**c1-19-031 · the captain requested that passengers should remain in their seats — KEEP.** Reader 1's *the passengers* needs a
second *the* (one card); the people cannot swap (*their seats*).

**c1-19-032 · This pair of glasses ___ ___ me over two hundred euros. — KEEP.** *pair* + singular verb is the map's C1 mistake
(*This pair of glasses are*); euros are the learners' currency.

**c1-19-036 · it's not the kind of job you can walk away from — ACCEPT** `it's the kind of job you can not walk away from`
(*can not* written apart = *cannot*; same meaning, preposition still at the end).

**c1-19-037 · the matter is being looked into by the police — KEEP.** The question order is already accepted.

**c1-19-038 · there's little hope ___ ___ the match now. — KEEP.** *hope to win* after the noun is the map's mistake; reader 1
calls it non-standard.

**c1-19-043 · ___ ___, the job isn't mine until I've signed the contract. — KEEP.** *Strictly speaking* is on the participle
card (Hewings); *Strictly spoken / Strict speaking* are the map's learner mistakes, so the form is tested, not only the phrase.

**c1-19-044 · no sooner had the film started than the power went off — KEEP.** The non-inverted order is already accepted.

**c1-19-045 · Opposite our office ___ two new cafés. — KEEP.** Only *are* fits; *there* would need a second gap.
