# Batch 4, point `each_every` (B1.2): notes of the writer

Topic: Subject and verb (`agreement`), level B1.2. Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*: unit 90 (*all, every and whole*: *all day* = the whole day, *every day* = on all days; *all day*, not *all the day*; *the whole* + singular noun, *the whole book* = *all the book*; *my whole life* = *all my life*) and unit 91 (*each and every*: often both are possible; *each* for two things, *each hand*; *each* alone and *each of* + *the / these / them*, *every one of*, not *every of*; *almost every*, *every single*, not *almost each*; *each / every* + singular verb).

Each and every are never set against each other where both fit: every gap where they compete is one where only one of them is possible (before *of*, two things, after *almost*). After *each of* + plural noun a plural verb is heard: no task makes the learner choose the verb there (the bank has no *have* chip; the order cards give *lives*; the gap asks for the noun).

## Mix (25, as in the brief)
7 choice, 5 typed gap, 3 whole sentence, 5 word order (1 question, *Put the words in order to make a question.*, which `lib.plain` shortens unless the lead's patch in `gap_points_b12.py` is active), 4 word bank, 1 matching (a learning task automatically).

## What the learner must know, task by task
Choice
1. *___ in our new hotel has a balcony…* → *Every room*: *every* + singular noun. Wrong: *Every rooms*, *All room*, *Each of room* (each one a mistake of this point). *Each room* is correct and is not an option.
2. *At the end of the course, each ___ a certificate…* → *student gets*: singular noun and singular verb. The three wrong options are the three other combinations of noun and verb, so both must be known.
3. *I've known Marta ___ because our mothers were best friends.* → *my whole life*. Wrong: *my all life* (the typical error), *whole my life*, *the whole my life*. *all my life* (correct) is not an option.
4. *…I stayed in bed ___ on Sunday.* → *all day*. Wrong: *every day* (one Sunday, so not on each day), *all the days*, *whole day* (no *the*). *the whole day* (correct) is not an option.
5. Which correct: *Each of the flats in our building has its own parking space.* Wrong: *Each of flats* (no *the*), *Every of the flats*, *Each the flats*.
6. NOT correct: *Every player in our club get a free shirt in spring.* (*every* + plural verb). The other three are correct and about three different things: *the whole village*, *all day*, *almost every shop … closes*.
7. *She was carrying a heavy bag in ___ hand…* → *each* (two hands). Wrong: *every* (Murphy 91: not for two), *all*, *both* (needs *hands*).

Typed gap (the instruction names two words where other words would otherwise fit)
8. *Write *each* or *every*: …three new apps…, and ___ of them was useful.* → *each* (*every* cannot go before *of*). Without the two words, *one*, *none*, *neither* would fit.
9. *Write *all* or *every*: The museum is open ___ day except Monday…* → *every* (*except Monday* means a set of days).
10. *Every laptop in our office ___ the same password, which is not very safe. (have)* → *has | has got* (*which is* fixes the present).
11. *Each of the ___ on our walking tour got a free map… (visitor)* → *visitors* (plural noun after *each of the*). The verb is a past form, so no singular / plural verb question.
12. *Write *all* or *whole*: The ___ team stayed late on Friday…* → *whole* (after *the*). Without the two words, *entire*, *new*, *sales* would fit.

Whole sentence
13. *Say it another way with *whole*: I spent all day at the airport because…* → *the whole day*, both clause orders.
14. *Say it another way with *every*: All the lessons at our language school last ninety minutes.* → *Every lesson … lasts*; also *every single lesson*, *every one of the lessons* (with *lasts* or *last*, as for *each of*), *90* as a number.
15. *Make a yes/no question: Every member of the team has access to the shared folder.* → *Does every member … have …?* and the British *Has every member … got access …?*

Word order
16. *each of my sisters lives in a different country so we talk online* (the card *lives* shows the singular verb).
17. (question) *does every guest need a ticket for tomorrow's boat trip*: only one article card, so no article swap.
18. *we were stuck in traffic all day and missed the start of the wedding*. *All day we were stuck…* was not added: with *and missed* it is not what people say.
19. *i missed the match because i spent the whole evening answering emails*, also with the *because* clause first. First draft *she spent the whole evening answering emails from her new clients* was dropped: the cards also gave *her whole evening … from the new clients* and *answering new emails*.
20. *i've read every single review but i can't decide which phone to buy*, also with the clauses swapped.

Word bank
21. *Each of my ___ ___ a different hobby…* [brothers, has, brother] → *brothers has*. *brother* is the *each of student* mistake. No *have* chip (a plural verb is heard after *each of*).
22. *I've had a headache ___ ___ morning…* [the, whole, every] → *the whole*; *whole the* is the typical mistake, *the every / every the* are wrong. No *all* chip, so *all the morning* cannot arise.
23. *My grandmother phones me ___ ___, usually just after her lunch…* [every, day, all, whole] → *every day*; *all day* contradicts *usually just after her lunch*; *whole day* needs *the*.
24. *Almost ___ colleague in my team works from home on Fridays…* [every, each, all] → *every*; *almost each* (Murphy 91) and *almost all colleague* are wrong.

Matching (learning)
25. *…each of* + *them brought a small gift.* / *…every* + *guest stayed until the end.* / *…all the* + *guests asked for the recipes.* / *…the whole* + *room got up to dance.* The endings differ in form (pronoun after *of*, singular noun, plural noun, noun for a group). Every swap gives at least one wrong sentence (*each of guest*, *every them*, *every guests*, *all the guest stayed*, *the whole guests*). The nearest case: *every room got up to dance* is grammatical, but then *the whole guest stayed* is wrong.

## Card lines needed (Subject and verb card)
The card has only *every* + verb-*s* (*Every student has a book.*). The tasks also use *each*, *each of*, *all day / every day* and *whole*. Proposed:
- use (EN): *each / every* + singular noun + verb-*s*: *Every room has a balcony. Each student gets a certificate.* *each of* + *the / my / them* + plural noun: *Each of the flats has a garden.* Not *every of*: *every one of them*.
- use (LV): *each / every* + lietvārds vienskaitlī + darbības vārds ar *-s*: *Every room has a balcony. Each student gets a certificate.* *each of* + *the / my / them* + daudzskaitlis: *Each of the flats has a garden.* Nevis *every of*: *every one of them*.
- use (EN): *all day* = the whole day; *every day* = on each day. *the whole* + noun: *the whole day, my whole life* (not *my all life*).
- use (LV): *all day* = visu dienu; *every day* = katru dienu. *the whole* + lietvārds: *the whole day, my whole life* (nevis *my all life*).
- watch (EN): two things: *each*, not *every*: *in each hand*. *almost every*, not *almost each*.
- watch (LV): divām lietām *each*, nevis *every*: *in each hand*. *almost every*, nevis *almost each*.

## Unsure / for the judge
- Task 4: *all the days* is a weaker distractor than the others, but it is wrong. *all the day* was avoided on purpose (heard, non-standard).
- Task 14: *Every one of the lessons … last* is accepted to match the lead's rule for *each of* + plural verb; Murphy 91 gives the singular.
- Task 15: *Has every member … got access …?* is accepted as the British *have got* question; the sentence itself has *has*.
- Task 7: a reader may say *both* is "nearly" right; it needs *hands*, so it is wrong as written.
