Will robots take our jobs?

Many experts believe that in the next twenty years, robots and artificial intelligence (AI) will change the way we work. According to a recent study, about one in five jobs will disappear by 2040. Jobs that are repeated every day, such as working in a factory or answering simple questions on the phone, are most at risk.

However, there is also good news. New technology will probably create new jobs, too. We will need people to build, program and repair the machines, and we will need experts to protect our data. Jobs that need creativity or care for people – such as teachers, nurses and designers – will probably stay safe, because robots cannot understand feelings the way humans can.

What can we do to get ready? Experts say that people are going to need to keep learning all their life. "The most important skill of the future is the ability to learn new skills," says professor Maija Kalna. Many companies are already training their workers: next month, a big Latvian bank is starting a free course in digital skills for 500 employees.

"I'm not afraid of robots," says Daniels, 24, a software developer. "I think they will make our lives easier. But I'm going to keep learning, because nobody knows what the future will bring."
