Local news: city launches 'Green Week'

Riga, 3 June. The city council announced yesterday that it will hold a "Green Week" from 10 to 16 June to encourage people to protect the environment. The mayor said that the aim was to reduce waste in the city by 15% in the next two years.

During the week, public transport will be free, so that fewer people will drive. In many parts of the city, people will be able to take their old electronics and clothes to special recycling points. "Last year we collected 12 tonnes of old clothes, and we hope to collect twice as much this year," said event manager Laura Bērziņa.

There will also be a number of events for children. Schools have been asked to organise clean-up days in nearby parks, and a famous Latvian cartoonist has promised to draw posters about recycling. The environment minister told reporters that the government was planning to ban plastic bags in supermarkets next year, and he asked everyone to bring their own bags.

Not everybody is happy. A taxi drivers' association has complained that free public transport will damage their business. However, the mayor said that the benefits for the environment were more important. "If we don't act now, we will have much bigger problems in the future," he said. Details of the programme can be found on the council's website.
