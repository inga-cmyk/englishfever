## r15-002 · B1.1 · gap · b1-new-5.csv:65

Task: Write one word that fits: I ___ drink coffee in the evening because it keeps me awake.
Key: {"kind": "gap", "accepted": [["never", "rarely", "hardly ever", "seldom", "don't", "do not", "can't", "cannot", "shouldn't", "should not"]]}
- reader 1: another correct answer, not accepted: [["mustn't"], ["won't"], ["wouldn't"], ['try not to']]
- reader 1: instruction: Open gap with no hint: many words fit (never, rarely, seldom, don't, can't, shouldn't, won't). 'because it keeps me awake' allows negative or reason-based modals. Needs a hint (e.g. frequency word) or restriction; 'one word' also excludes 'hardly ever' / 'do not' inconsistently.
- reader 2: another correct answer, not accepted: [["mustn't"], ["won't"], ['try not to']]
- reader 2: instruction: Open gap: many words fit (never, rarely, seldom, can't, shouldn't, won't, mustn't). Nothing says which kind of word is wanted (negative auxiliary? adverb?). 'one word' makes 'don't' a contraction and rules out 'do not'. Needs a clue or a fixed form. Also 'try not to' etc. are not one word, so ignore them.
- reader 2: level: Fine for B1.1, even easy (simple present negative is A1-A2).
- reader 2: sense: Sentence is natural and logical; 'never/rarely' give a slightly different meaning but are correct.

## r15-003 · B1.1 · gap · b1-tenses-2.csv:122

Task: The film ___ at eight, so let's meet at seven. (start)
Key: {"kind": "gap", "accepted": [["starts", "will start", "is starting"]]}
- reader 1: another correct answer, not accepted: [['is going to start']]
- reader 1: instruction: No instruction (what to do with the word in brackets, which form). Present simple, present continuous, will and going to all fit; only timetable meaning favours 'starts', and nothing in the task says that. Name the tense or change the context.
- reader 2: another correct answer, not accepted: [['begins'], ['is going to start'], ['is due to start'], ['starts at']]
- reader 2: instruction: The cue (start) is given but not the form wanted. Present simple 'starts' is the intended timetable use, but 'is starting', 'will start', 'is going to start' (and 'is due to start') are also correct English for a future event. Without a note such as 'timetable' or 'use the present simple', several answers are possible. Also does not make clear if 'start' may be changed.
- reader 2: level: Timetable present simple for future is fine for B1.1.
- reader 2: sense: Natural and logical.
