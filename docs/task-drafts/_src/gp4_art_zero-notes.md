# Batch 4, point `art_zero` (A2.1), topic Articles: notes

**Murphy:** *English Grammar in Use*, unit 75 (no article for things in general: plural and uncountable nouns, also sports and school subjects) and unit 77 (names with and without *the* 1: people, cities, most countries; *the UK, the USA*). I did not use units 70, 73, 74 or 76. Meals and *go to work* (unit 74, `art_places`), languages and *children / the children* (`art_general`) are left out on purpose.

**Mix:** 7 choice, 5 word order, 4 word bank, 1 matching (learning), 5 typed gaps, 3 whole sentence. This is the brief's mix.

**How the risk "*the* is correct when a particular thing is meant" was handled:** every sentence is about a name (people, cities, most countries), a sport or a school subject (*play the golf* and *teaches the maths* are impossible), or it makes the general meaning certain with a habit word (*usually, in the morning, with every meal*), a comparison between kinds (*than cats, than laptops, than cars*), *even small ones*, *my favourite flowers*, a hobby (*collects*), or one situation sentence (*I'm vegetarian.*). I added *a / an* as an option only where nobody would write it as correct English. I never used it with *coffee / tea / water*, because *a coffee* is correct.

**Typed gaps:** a learner cannot type "no article" (the grader has no "—"). So each typed gap gives a singular noun in brackets, and the answer is the plural with no article (*spiders, bikes, tablets, roses, stamps*). The mistake that is refused is *the spiders* (or *spider*). *are* or *ones* in the sentence rules out the general singular *a tablet / a rose*.

## Task by task: what the learner must know, and why the wrong options compete
1. *We went to ___ Croatia last summer.* (— / the / a): country names take no article. *the* is the overgeneralised article; *a* is the "every noun needs one" mistake.
2. *My sister teaches ___ maths in Riga.*: a school subject takes no article. *the maths* is the typical mistake.
3. *I'm vegetarian. I don't eat ___ meat.*: the situation is needed (cover test). Without it, *I don't eat the meat* (on this plate) is correct.
4. *Which sentence is correct?* Peter / Canada: no article with a person's name or a country. The wrong options put *the* on one name or on both.
5. *Which sentence is correct?* *plays golf*: a sport takes no article. The wrong options are *the golf* (the instrument pattern *play the piano*) and *a golf*.
6. *NOT correct:* *My parents live in the Spain.* The three correct sentences are about three different things: an uncountable noun (*fish*), a general plural (*old cameras*) and the exception *the UK* (allowed by the lead as a correct sentence only).
7. *NOT correct:* *She studies the history at university.* Correct: a city name, a general plural (*horses*), an uncountable noun (*milk*).
8. Order *i drink green tea in the morning* (+ *in the morning…*): if the *the* card moves (*the green tea in morning*), the sentence is wrong, so *the* has only one place.
9. Order *my grandfather doesn't like computers*: a general plural, no other order.
10. Order *my wife and i met in paris*: a city name.
11. Order *do you like classical music* (question): an uncountable noun in general.
12. Order *we want to visit japan next year* (+ *next year…*): a country name.
   (Items 9 to 12 have no article card, so they practise the pattern rather than test the choice. That is the nature of a word order with no extra cards.)
13. Bank *Our new manager is from ___ Sweden.* (— / the).
14. Bank *I usually listen to ___ jazz in the car.* (— / the): *usually* makes it a habit, so *the jazz* has nothing particular to refer to.
15. Bank *I think ___ dogs are better pets than cats.* (— / the): the comparison is between kinds (*than cats* has no article), so *the dogs* does not fit.
16. Bank *My kids spend hours on ___ social media.* (— / the): *on the social media* is a frequent learner mistake.
   I left out *a* in the banks because *a Sweden* or *a social media* is not a form anyone writes (rule of 8 November).
17. Matching (learning): *lives in = Rome / drink = orange juice / afraid of = snakes / play = volleyball*. Every swap gives nonsense.
18. *afraid of ___, even small ones. (spider)*: *ones* and *even small ones* force the plural and the general meaning.
19. *In big cities, ___ are often faster than cars. (bike)*: a general comparison of kinds.
20. *___ are cheaper than laptops. (tablet)*: as 19.
21. *___ are my favourite flowers. (rose)*: *favourite flowers* are kinds, so the general plural.
22. *My son collects ___. He has hundreds. (stamp)*: a hobby means the things in general; *hundreds* forces the plural.
23. *Correct the mistake: My uncle works in the London.* → *My uncle works in London.*
24. *Correct the mistake: We play the basketball on Fridays.* → also *On Fridays we play basketball.*
25. *Correct the mistake: I eat the bread with every meal.* → *with every meal* makes it general. *a bread* does not exist, so there is only one correction.
