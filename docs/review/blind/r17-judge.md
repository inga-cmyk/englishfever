**(1) Rebuilt, need round r17b (4 tasks):**
- **r17-009** (ip-B12-2a, *Could you tell me where ___ ?*): both readers took *you have bought that jacket* as only less natural, so the wrong option was not clearly wrong. Now *Could you tell me when ___ ? The guarantee may still be valid.*: **you bought this laptop** / did you buy / did you bought / you have bought this laptop. With *when*, the present perfect is wrong (Murphy 13: *When did you…?*, not *When have you…?*). The why line says so. The report_fix call of 21 November was changed.
- **r17-012** (ia-C11-6a, write, *The question is whether / how / why*): both readers said there was no situation, so any sentence would do. Now it starts with a situation: *Your team wants to launch the new app next month, but the tests are not finished, and a delay will cost money.* The model (*The question is whether we can afford the delay.*) answers it, and the four checks are the same. The report_fix call of 21 November was changed.
- **r17-026** (c-2361, *It was a difficult task, and ___ they finished it on time.*): the open gap also took *somehow, luckily, fortunately, finally, surprisingly* (both readers). That breaks the rule of 3 November (no open gap that several words fit). Now a choice: **yet** / although / despite / in spite of. *and although they finished it on time.* leaves the sentence unfinished, and *despite* and *in spite of* cannot go before a clause. `replaces(old task text)`. The *nevertheless / nonetheless* overlay entry was removed (it no longer matches anything).
- **r17-051** (*I'm not sure ___ ___ ___ ___ the tickets yet.*): with the card *did*, the cards also built *if we did book the tickets yet* (emphatic, American with *yet*). Both readers called it odd but not clearly wrong. The card *did* is now *do*: *if do we book* keeps the question-order mistake, and *if we do book … yet* is not English. The row is new today, so it was replaced in place.

**(2) Answers added** (`record-current r17`):
- **r17-019:** *after lunch i don't usually / usually don't drink coffee because it keeps me awake* (both readers; the time phrase at the front). Not added: *…because it keeps me awake after lunch*, which changes the meaning.
- **r17-020:** the cards also make the opposite facts, *my parents never read a newspaper but they watch the news every day*, and nothing in the task fixes which is true (both readers; reader 1 answered with it). Four orders of that sentence were added (subject first, *every day* first, clauses swapped). The point (*never*, *every day*, present simple) is the same in both.
- **r17-023:** *When she sold the café, she had had it since 2010.* (both readers), and the forms with *'d had* and with the *when* clause second. Answer note: *have* (= own) is a state verb too, so *had had*.
- **r17-025:** *That flat is less expensive than this.* (and *That flat's…*). *this* as a pronoun for a thing is correct. *this one* is more usual.
- **r17-030:** *the night bus will run*, with a note: it asks about the future (a new timetable). For the timetable now, *runs*.
- **r17-032:** *Do you know where I can print this?* (both readers), with *Excuse me* and *please*.
- **r17-038:** *if / whether our room will have*, with a note: *will have* asks about the room you will be given. *has* asks about the room as it is.
- **r17-044:** *Could you tell me where the shop is / that shop is?* (both readers; the instruction says *Ask her about the shop*).
- **r17-050:** *Do you know if the cleaner(s) found / has (have) found / took / has (have) taken it / my umbrella?*
- **r17-056:** *The house had been owned by my grandparents for decades before we bought it.* (both readers; it keeps *had*), and the form with *Before we bought it* first. Note: the passive of *own* is correct; the mistake was *had been belonging*. `twins-2.csv` row 49 was edited by hand in the same way as `twins_2.py`.

**(3) Kept:**
- **r17-001** (*Why do we say 'Tickets are sold here'?*): *Someone is selling tickets right now* reads *are sold* as an action going on now (that would be *are being sold*). It is the believable misunderstanding that the rules of 21 November give as their example. Meaning questions are learning tasks, and the use of the passive is on the A2.2 card.
- **r17-002:** the reader calls the level acceptable. The third conditional at B1.2 is on the map, and a matching task is never evidence.
- **r17-003:** all the reports are already accepted (*had to / must / would have to / to finish*). *I should finish by Friday* is not accepted: *should* is advice and changes the meaning of *must* (rule of 4 November).
- **r17-011:** a write task about a real need is open by design. Its checks look for the polite start, *if* or a question word, and statement order, and they accept every question the readers suggested.
- **r17-018, r17-024:** the readers' orders are already accepted (nested list).
- **r17-022:** *had had* is the key. *had been having* stays wrong (*have* = own).
- **r17-029, 031, 041, 045, 048:** both readers gave the key.
- **r17-039:** *Can you tell me does the waiter speak English?* is the mistake the card teaches (Murphy 50). The reader calls it non-standard.
- **r17-040** (*Do you know where ___ the car last night? (Tom / park)*): *Tom had parked* is not accepted. The past perfect needs a later past moment (Murphy 15), and here the only time is *last night*, when the parking itself happened. *Tom parked* is the form.
- **r17-046:** *…when you sent it? We still haven't received it.* leaves *it* with nothing before it to refer to. The correction keeps *the invoice*.
- **r17-054** (*Ask politely, starting with Do you know: When does the shop close?*): *when the shop will close / is closing* stay out. The task turns the given question into a polite one, and the card says the tense does not change (Murphy 50: *What time does the film start?* → *what time the film starts*).
