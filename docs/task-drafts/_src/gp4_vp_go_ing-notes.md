## Decisions on correct English (so that no wrong option is correct somewhere)
- **No *on* before *a walk / a run / a swim*.** *go on a walk* is common in American English and is also used in British English. So *on* is never a wrong option there, and the typed gap *We went ___ a long walk* accepts *for | on*. The map's typical error *go on a walk* is therefore not used as a mistake.
- **No *on* before -ing.** *go on shopping / go on fishing* means "continue shopping" and is correct English. The lead's typical error *go on shopping* is not used.
- **No *to* + verb as a wrong option** (*went to ski*, *goes to dance*). It is correct English with a purpose meaning.
- **No *to* before *swimming / dancing / running*.** In British English *go to swimming* can mean a class, so it is borderline. *to* + -ing is only used as a wrong option with *fishing, skiing, camping, shopping*, where it is clearly wrong.
- **No *for* as a wrong option before *a trip*.** *go for a trip* is rare but readers may defend it. The wrong options before *a trip* are *to* and *—*. Before bare *holiday*, *for holiday* is wrong in British English, so it stays.
- Equivalent forms are accepted, never set against each other: *go swimming = go for a swim*, *go running = go for a run*. *go to the shops* and *do some shopping* are accepted in the *shopping* rewrite. *go into work* (British English) is accepted next to *go to work*.
- Latvian: no *Tulkojot burtiski* note. *go to home* and *go to shopping* are general learner mistakes, and Latvian *iet mājās* does not clearly cause them. Every Latvian word in the explanations is in the italics word list, so no extra `LV_BY_FILE` entry is needed.

## Task by task: what must the learner know, and why do the wrong options compete
Choice
1. *We often go ___ fishing at the lake.* (— / to / for) The learner must know that *go* + -ing takes nothing in between. *go to fishing* and *go for fishing* are the two typical mistakes.
2. *Let's go ___ a coffee after the meeting.* (for / to / on / —) The learner must know *go for a* + noun. *to* is the Latvian "uz" reflex, *on* comes from *go on holiday*, and *—* comes from *go fishing*.
3. *They're going ___ holiday to Greece in May.* (on / to / for / in) The learner must know *go on holiday*. *to holiday* and *for holiday* are typical mistakes, and *in* comes from "atvaļinājumā".
4. *Do you want to go ___ before breakfast?* (for a swim / for swimming / to swimming / for a swimming) The learner must know that *for* needs *a* + noun. The wrong options mix the two correct patterns. *swimming* alone (also correct) is not offered.
5. *Which sentence is correct?* *We went skiing …* against *to skiing / for skiing / for a skiing*. The learner must know -ing with no word in between. Each wrong option is a different mix of patterns.
6. *Which sentence is NOT correct?* *Shall we go to shopping on Saturday?* The other three are correct and about three different patterns (*go to the gym*, *go for a walk*, *go on a trip*).
7. *Which sentence is NOT correct?* *I went to home early yesterday.* The other three are correct and different (*go running*, *go for a drink*, *go to bed*).

Typed gap
8. *I usually go ___ bed at eleven.* The only answer is *to*. The learner must know *go to bed*, with no *the*.
9. *Where are you going ___ holiday this year?* The only answer is *on*.
10. *What time do you go ___ work?* The answers are *to | into*. *go into work* is British English and is accepted.
11. *We went ___ a long walk on the beach.* The answers are *for | on*. The learner must know *go for a* + noun, and *on a walk* is accepted (see above).
12. *She goes ___ in the park every morning. (run)* The answers are *running | for a run*. *run* is also a noun, so both readings are accepted (rule of the verb-and-noun hint). *goes run* is not possible with *goes*, even in American English.

Whole sentence
13. *Say it another way with *for*: Let's go swimming after work.* The answer is *go for a swim*. The learner must change -ing into *a* + noun. *go for swimming* is the trap.
14. *Say it another way with *shopping*: Let's go to the shops this afternoon.* The answer is *go shopping* (the trap is *go to shopping*). *do some / the shopping* and *Shall we …?* are also accepted.
15. *Say it another way with *for*: I took a short walk after lunch.* The answer is *went for a short walk*. The learner must use the past of *go* together with *for a* + noun. *went out for* is also accepted.

Word order (alternatives: the time phrase first)
16. *we go cycling every saturday*: *go* + -ing with nothing in between.
17. *my dad goes for a bike ride every sunday*: *for a* + noun. This is a new noun, so it is not copied from another task.
18. *she goes to the gym before work*: *to* + place.
19. *we're going on holiday next week*: *on holiday*. *we're* is one card, so no question order can be built from the cards.
20. *he went home after the meeting*: *home* with no *to*.

Word bank (the extra chips are the point's own mistakes, real words, never rejectable without the point)
21. *We went ___ a boat trip on the river.* (on / to / —)
22. *Last summer we went ___ in the forest.* (camping / to camping / for camping)
23. *It's late, so I'm going ___.* (home / to home / at home). *at home* is the "mājās" reflex.
24. *Let's go ___ a ___ before dinner.* (for, run / to, running). I checked every combination: only *for a run* is correct. *on* is left out on purpose.

Matching (a learning task)
25. *Let's go for / She goes to / We're going on / They often go* + *a coffee … / the gym … / holiday … / fishing …*. I swapped every pair of endings and none gives a correct sentence. *going on fishing* would mean "continue", and it makes no sense with *at the weekend*.

## Card lines needed (Verb patterns card, "Watch out", after the existing *go* + -ing line)
- EN: *go for a* + noun: *go [[for]] a walk, a run, a swim, a coffee* (*go for a swim* = *go swimming*). *go to* + place: *go [[to]] the gym, to work, to bed*. *go [[on]] holiday / a trip*. *go [[home]]* (no *to*).
- LV: *go for a* + lietvārds: *go [[for]] a walk, a run, a swim, a coffee* (*go for a swim* = *go swimming*). *go to* + vieta: *go [[to]] the gym, to work, to bed*. *go [[on]] holiday / a trip*. *go [[home]]* (bez *to*).
