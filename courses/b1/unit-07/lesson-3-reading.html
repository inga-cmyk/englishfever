📖 Unit 7 · Reading (exam practice)

💡 Exam tips

  1. Read the title and look at the text quickly.
  2. Read each question. Then find the part of the text that answers it.
  3. Check all the options (a, b, c). Wrong options often use words from the text, but they say something different.

📄 Can we trust the news?

Twenty years ago, most people got their news from television, radio or newspapers. Today, more than half of young people say that social media is their main source of news. This has changed the media world completely.

The biggest problem with online news is "fake news". A recent study found that false stories travel six times faster on social media than true ones. Some stories are written as jokes, but others are made to deceive people on purpose. "People often share a headline without reading the article," said media expert Dr Anna Vilks. "They said they didn't have time, but it took them only a minute to share it."

So how can you check if a story is true? Experts advise you to ask these questions: Who wrote the article? Does the website have a good reputation? Do other reliable sources report the same story? If the headline makes you very angry or very excited, you should be careful, because fake stories are often written to create strong emotions.

Many schools now teach "media literacy" lessons. In one Latvian school, the teacher told the students to find a false story online and explain how they knew it was false. "It was a great exercise," one student said. "I'll never share a story without checking it again."
