# Batch 4, point `if_when` (B1.1): notes of the writer

Source: `docs/task-drafts/_src/gp4_if_when.py`, topic *First conditional (if + present, will)* (`cond_first`), level B1.1.
Grammar: Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, unit 25 (C: *when* = I'm sure it will happen, *if* = it is possible, it may happen; A-B: *when / if* + present simple for the future, not *will*; *when I've finished* is possible too).

Design rule for the whole set: without a cue, *if* and *when* are both correct in almost every sentence (the speaker's certainty is open). So every task where the learner chooses between them has one short sentence or phrase that makes the event sure (a timetable, a plan, a date: *gets in at six*, *We're getting married in June*, *I'm having lunch with Tom today*) or not sure (*may not*, *might*, *Perhaps*, *It's not certain yet*). This is the case of the cover test where the situation is the task. Every event is one future event, never something that happens every time (no zero-conditional *when*).

## Mix (25, as in the brief)
7 choice, 5 typed gap, 3 whole sentence, 5 word order, 4 word bank, 1 matching (a learning task automatically).

## What the learner must know, task by task
Choice (four of the seven have the same 2 x 2 grid: *if / when* x present / *will*, so the wrong options compete on both parts of the point)
1. *My train gets in at six. ___, I'll call you from the station.* → *When I arrive*. The timetable makes the arrival sure; *If I arrive* doubts it; *When / If I will arrive* is the *will* mistake.
2. *I may not get the job, but ___, I'll move to Oslo.* → *if I get it*. *may not* makes it uncertain; *when I get it* contradicts it.
3. Which sentence is correct? *The film ends at nine, so I'll call you when it finishes.* Against *if it finishes* (the film surely ends) and *when / if it will finish*.
4. *My brother might visit us next month. ___, we'll take him to the new museum.* → *If he comes*. The result is a one-off (the museum), so *when* cannot be read as "every time he comes".
5. NOT correct: *I'll text you when I will land in Riga.* (*will* after *when*). The other three are correct and about three different things: weather / a walk (*if*), London next week (*when*), help with the move (*if* + an order).
6. *We're getting married in June. ___ married, we'll look for a bigger flat.* → *When we're*. A planned wedding is sure; *If we're* doubts it; *When / If we'll be* is the *will* mistake.
7. *___ my visa in time, I'll fly to Toronto in May. It's not certain yet.* → *If I get*. Here the cue comes after the sentence.

Typed gap
8. *Write *if* or *when*: I'm having lunch with Tom today. ___ I see him, …* → *When* (the lunch is arranged). The instruction names the two words because *Once / As soon as / After* would also fit an open gap.
9. *When the update ___, the app will restart by itself. (finish)* → *finishes | has finished | 's finished | is finished* (Murphy 25: the present perfect is possible after *when*; *is finished* is correct English too).
10. *Write *if* or *when*: The bank might be closed on Saturday. ___ it is, …* → *If*.
11. *My course starts on Monday. When it ___, I'll have less free time. (start)* → *starts | has started | 's started*. The mistake it is about: *will start*.
12. *Write *if* or *when*: The museum opens at ten. ___ it opens, …* → *When*.

Whole sentence
13. *Join with *if* or *when*: My contract ends in June. Then I'll take a long holiday.* → *When my contract ends (in June), I'll take a long holiday.* Both clause orders, with and without *in June*, *I'll / I will*, *has ended*, and *In June, when …* / *… in June, when …* are accepted. The learner chooses *when* and changes nothing else but *will* → present.
14. *Join with *if* or *when*: Perhaps the hotel is full. In that case, we'll stay with friends.* → *If the hotel is full, we'll stay with friends.* Both orders, *hotel's*, *we will*, *then*, *instead* accepted.
15. *Join with *when*: I'll finish this email. Then I'll call you back.* → *When I finish / I've finished / I have finished this email, I'll call you back*, both orders. Not copying: *I'll finish* must become *I finish*.

Word order (two questions, three statements; only one of *if / when* is in the cards, so these practise the form and the clause order)
16. *what will you do when you finish your degree* (question) / also *when you finish your degree what will you do*.
17. *will you text me when your plane lands in rome* (question) / also *when your plane lands in rome will you text me*. The cards also make the statement *you will text me when …*: with the patched *to make a question* only the questions are accepted (the current `od()` still adds the statements; see below).
18. *if the meeting finishes early i'll go to the gym* / also the result first. *early* at the end changes the meaning and is not natural.
19. *i'll send you the photos of the flat when i get home* / also *when* first. (A first version had *photos from the trip*: the cards also gave *when I get home from the trip*, so the phrase was changed.)
20. *my parents will move to the coast when they retire* / also *when they retire my parents will …* and *when my parents retire they will move to the coast* (same cards, natural). *they will move … when my parents retire* is not accepted: *they* would be other people.

Word bank (the wrong chips are *if / when* of this point and the *will* form, never agreement mistakes)
21. *My new laptop arrives on Friday. ___ it ___, I'll set it up for you.* [When, If, arrives, will arrive] → *When / arrives*.
22. *I might not finish the report today. ___ I ___ it, I'll send it to you tonight.* [If, When, finish, will finish] → *If / finish*.
23. *We're moving to Riga in March. ___ there, we'll see each other more often.* [When we're, If we're, When we'll be] → *When we're*.
24. *I might get tickets for the concert. ___ I ___ them, I'll take you with me.* [If, When, get, will get] → *If / get*.

Matching (learning)
25. *Match each sentence with the one that follows it.* *My flight lands at six. = When it does, I'll text you.* / *My flight might be late. = If it is, I'll take a taxi.* / *I'm not sure the hotel has parking. = If it doesn't, we'll park at the station.* / *Our room will be ready at two. = When it is, we'll take our bags up.* The short answers (*does / is / doesn't / is*) and the content fix one pairing; each sure sentence goes with *when*, each unsure one with *if*. Swap test: *lands at six* + *If it is* has no *is* to repeat; *might be late* + *When it does* has no *does*; *room ready at two* + *If it is, I'll take a taxi* and *parking* + *When it does, I'll text you* make no sense.

## Card lines needed
The card already has the *if / when* line in *Watch out* (*if* = maybe it happens; *when* = it will happen). Two small additions would make the card cover every task here:
- Watch out, second line, add *when* to the list (tasks 5, 9, 11, 15 and the banks are about *will* after *when*):
  - EN: *No *will* after *when, as soon as, before, until* either: *I'll call you when I [[get]] home.**
  - LV: *Arī aiz *when, as soon as, before, until* nav *will*: *I'll call you when I [[get]] home.**
- Optional, Watch out (the certainty cue, for tasks 1, 6, 13): 
  - EN: *Something sure takes *when*: *My train gets in at six. [[When]] I arrive, I'll call you.* Not sure: *I might go. [[If]] I go, I'll call you.**
  - LV: *Drošam notikumam lieto *when*: *My train gets in at six. [[When]] I arrive, I'll call you.* Nedrošam: *I might go. [[If]] I go, I'll call you.**

## Doubts
- Task 21 (*My new laptop arrives on Friday*): a delivery date is presented as fixed, so *If it arrives* is wrong by Murphy 25C; a reader might argue deliveries are never certain. If the judge flags it, *The shop is delivering my new laptop on Friday* makes it stronger.
- Task 6 (*If we're married*): wrong only by the plan in the first sentence; the cover test applies on purpose.
- The two question orders depend on the lead's patch: `od()` now still adds the statements (*you will text me when …*) and `plain()` still shortens the instruction to *Put the words in order.*
