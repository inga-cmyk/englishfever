## Task by task: what the learner must know, and why the wrong options compete
1. *Which sentence has a mistake?* **Our kitchen chairs were made from my uncle.** The doer (a person) comes after *by*; *made from* is for the material (*made from wood*), so *from my uncle* is the believable mistake (the map's typical error *painted from my grandfather*). The three correct sentences are about three different things: *Breakfast is served from seven to ten* (present passive, no doer, and a correct *from*, which makes the learner look at what follows *from*), *My grandmother was born in a small town* (past passive, no doer), *The museum is visited by thousands of tourists* (present passive with *by*).
2. Typed gap *Our hotel is owned ___ a Swedish company.* → *by*. Chosen because no other one word fits after *owned* before a company (*owned for / with / of* are not English), so the open gap has one answer.
3. Word bank *In our village, the bread is baked ___ a local family.* (by / from / of) → *by*. *from* competes because bread *is baked from flour*; *of* is the Latvian genitive pattern (*vietējās ģimenes cepta*). Neither builds a correct sentence with *a local family*. *for* and *with* were left out on purpose: *baked for / with a local family* are correct English.
4. Typed gaps *Write one word in each gap: The windows of our office are ___ ___ a small company every month. (clean)* → *cleaned / by*. The learner must give the 3rd form after *are* and add the doer with *by*. *for* in the second gap would make no sense with *our office*. (Rebuilt after blind check r13: the first version, *The windows of our office ___ ___ a small company every month.*, needed three words in two gaps, so a reader split it *are / cleaned by*, which the grader cannot key, and *every month* also left *were cleaned* open.)
5. Whole sentence *Start with *This bridge*: A Latvian engineer designed this bridge.* → *This bridge was designed by a Latvian engineer.* The start makes the passive necessary without naming it; the doer is new information, so *by a Latvian engineer* must stay. The start does not contain the answer.

Settings: furniture / home, hotel / business, village food, office work, a bridge (travel). Not the same setting twice in a row.

## Doubts

Here the doer is always one that matters, and the explanations say so. If the teacher wants that half tested, it would have to be a learning task (a meaning question).
