# Batch 4, point `rel_what` (B1.2): notes of the writer

Topic: Relative clauses (`relative_clauses`). Murphy, *English Grammar in Use*, unit 93, section D. It says to use *Everything (that) they said was true*, not *Everything what*, and that *what* = the thing(s) that (*Did you hear what they said? What happened was my fault*). Unit 92 covers *that / which* after a noun. Rule: *what* has no noun before it. After a noun use *that / which*, or nothing when it is the object. After *everything / something / anything / all* use *that* or nothing, never *what*. *which* after *everything* is rare (Murphy: "not usually"), so no task makes the learner choose it or reject it. Where a correction could produce it, it is accepted.

Two design rules that apply to every task:
- **Nothing (zero) is never a wrong option where it is correct.** After a noun or *everything* the object pronoun can be left out, so "—" is a wrong option only where *what* is needed (*I didn't hear you said*, *That's exactly I was trying*). In the word banks a chip must be used, so zero cannot come up.
- **No *which* as a wrong option where it could mean "which one".** *I can't remember which he chose* is correct English. *which* is a wrong option only where it cannot be read that way: as a subject (*Which I need is…*), after *That's exactly* or *This isn't*, or in front of a verb that has an object (*hear*).

## Mix (25, as in the brief)
7 choice, 5 typed gap, 3 whole sentence, 5 word order (1 question), 4 word bank, 1 matching (a learning task automatically).

## What the learner must know, task by task
Choice
1. *Sorry, I didn't hear ___ you said about the meeting time.* [what / that / —]. No noun before the gap and *said* needs an object, so *what*. *that* is the Latvian *ko* → *that* mistake, and *—* is ungrammatical here. *which* was left out (see above).
2. *The jacket ___ I ordered online last week doesn't fit me.* [which / what / who / where]. After a thing noun use *which*. *what* is the main mistake of this point. *that* is not offered because it is also correct.
3. Which correct: *Everything that he told us about the hotel was true.* against *Everything what…* and *All what…*.
4. Which correct: *What I really need right now is a long holiday.* against *That I…*, *Which I…*, *The thing what I…*. Each wrong option is a way to say "the thing that" without *what*.
5. NOT correct: *The film what we watched last night was far too long.* The other three are correct and each shows something different: *what* after a verb (*couldn't believe what I saw*), *everything* + nothing (*Everything she cooked*), *what* in a request (*Tell me what you think*). The option audit flags "odd theme". That is the NOT-correct rule (three different things), so it is not a problem.
6. NOT correct: *All what I want for my birthday is a quiet dinner.* The other three: a noun + *that* (*The shoes that I use*), *What* as the subject (*What surprised me most*), *everything* + nothing.
7. *That's exactly ___ I was trying to explain to my boss.* [what / that / which / —]. Only *what* is possible.

Typed gap. The instruction is *Write *what* or *that*:* because without it *all / everything / something* fit some of these gaps (*I can't remember all the teacher said*, *Did you hear something happened*).
8. *The pizza ___ we ordered last night…* → *that* (noun before it).
9. *I can't remember ___ the teacher said about the exam.* → *what*.
10. *Is there anything ___ I can do to help you with the move?* → *that* (after *anything*).
11. *___ worries me most about the trip is the cost of the flights.* → *What* (subject = the thing that).
12. *The app ___ my sister recommended…* → *that*.

Whole sentence
13. *Say it another way with *what*: The thing I enjoy most about my job is meeting new people.* → *What I enjoy most about my job is meeting new people.* and also *Meeting new people is what I enjoy most about my job.*
14. *Correct the mistake: Everything what she told me about the course was useful.* → *Everything that / which / — she told me…*, and *What she told me about the course was useful* (also a correct fix). The grader also accepts the corrected word alone.
15. *Correct the mistake: I didn't understand that the mechanic said about my car.* → *what the mechanic said*, plus *everything (that) / all (that) the mechanic said*. I used *said*, not *told me*, on purpose: *I didn't understand that the mechanic told me about my car* is a correct sentence (*that* as a conjunction).

Word order
16. *what she said at the wedding surprised everyone*. Also accepted: *what she said surprised everyone at the wedding* and *at the wedding what she said surprised everyone*.
17. *i didn't understand what the doctor told me about the test*. The people cannot be swapped (*me*).
18. *all i want after work is a hot shower* (*all* + nothing). Also accepted: *after work all i want is a hot shower* and *all i want is a hot shower after work*.
19. *i'm sorry about everything that happened last night*. I took *really* out because it could move (*everything that really happened*).
20. *Put the words in order to make a question.* *did you find what you were looking for*. These cards cannot make a statement. Note: `plain()` in `lib.py` writes the instruction as *Put the words in order.* in the CSV. Nothing is lost, because no statement is possible.

Word bank. The wrong chips are the mistakes of this point.
21. *___ really annoyed me was the noise from the street.* [What, That, Which] → *What*.
22. *I'll send you everything ___ you need for the conference.* [that, what] → *that*. I gave only one wrong chip: a third relative word would be either correct (*which*) or a word of another kind.
23. *The best thing about the trip was the people ___ we met on the way.* [that, what, which] → *that*. *which* is wrong because the noun is people. *who* is not a chip because it is also correct.
24. *Excuse me, this isn't ___ I ordered.* [what, that, which] → *what*. The first draft had a second sentence (*I asked for the fish…*). The cover test showed it was not needed, so it is gone.

Matching (learning)
25. *Thank you so much for everything / you did for my family*; *The waiter didn't understand / what I wanted to order*; *The laptop that I bought last month / stopped working after a week*; *What I like most about my job / is the people I work with*. Swap test: every swap is ungrammatical (*for everything what I wanted*, *didn't understand you did*) or makes no sense (*The laptop … is the people I work with*).

## Card line needed (the card has only *The book what I bought* among its mistakes)
- EN: *what* = the thing(s) that, with no noun before it: *I didn't hear what he said. What I need is a holiday.* After a noun or *everything / something / all*, use *that* (or nothing), not *what*: *Everything (that) he said was true.*
- LV: *what* = tas, ko / tas, kas (pirms tā nav lietvārda): *I didn't hear what he said. What I need is a holiday.* Aiz lietvārda vai *everything / something / all* lieto *that* (vai neko), nevis *what*: *Everything (that) he said was true.*

## Doubts
- Choice 2 accepts *which* and does not offer *that*. A reader may say "*that* is also correct". That is true, but it is not one of the options, so the key stands.
- Order 16 accepts *at the wedding what she said surprised everyone* (a place phrase first). Natural with a comma. Drop it if the judge finds it forced.
- Order 20 is 7 words, a little short for B1.2. A longer version (*…at the market*) gets a second order (*did you find at the market what…*).
