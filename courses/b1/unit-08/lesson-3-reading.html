Black Friday: good or bad?

Every November, shops across the world offer big discounts on a day called "Black Friday". The tradition began in the United States, but it has become popular in Latvia too. Last year, Latvians spent about 150 million euros during the Black Friday week, and many shops were open until midnight.

For many customers, Black Friday is a chance to find bargains. "I wait all year to buy a new phone on Black Friday," says Oskars, 27. "If I buy it on a normal day, I will pay at least 150 euros more." Online shops are especially popular because you don't have to stand in a queue.

However, economists warn that not all discounts are real. A consumer study found that in one third of cases, the 'old price' had been increased before Black Friday so that the discount looked bigger. The study also showed that many people buy things that they don't need. "If you can't afford something, a discount won't make it a bargain," said consumer expert Ruta Kalnina.

Her advice is to make a budget before you go shopping, to compare prices in different shops and to keep the receipt in case you want a refund. If you follow these tips, you can enjoy the sales without spending too much.
