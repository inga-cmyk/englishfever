Unit 4 · Writing: informal and formal emails

Goal: write informal and formal emails about work and studies, using modal verbs and linking words.

1. Formal or informal?

We choose our words for the reader. We write informal emails to friends and family. We write formal emails to a boss, a teacher, a company or someone we do not know.

Informal (friends)Formal (work, school, companies)
Hi Anna, / Dear Anna,Dear Mr Brown, / Dear Ms Smith, / Dear Sir or Madam,
Thanks for your email. / Great to hear from you!Thank you for your email. / I am writing to ask about ...
Can you ...? / Could you ...?Could you please ...? / I would be grateful if you could ...
I'm sorry, but I can't ...Unfortunately, I am unable to ...
Write soon! / See you! / Take care,I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your help.
Love, / Best wishes, / Bye for now,Yours sincerely, (name known) / Yours faithfully, (name not known)

Tips: In informal emails you can use short forms (I'm, can't), phrasal verbs and exclamation marks. In formal emails use full forms (I am, cannot), polite words (please, would, could) and no slang.

2. A good email has four parts

  1. Greeting – Dear ... / Hi ...
  2. Reason – why you are writing (1 sentence)
  3. Main part – the details, one idea in each paragraph
  4. Ending – a closing sentence and a sign-off

3. Coherence and cohesion

Coherence means your ideas are in a clear order and the reader can follow them. Use paragraphs. Put one idea in each paragraph.

Cohesion means the sentences are joined with small words. Use linking words:

UseLinking words
addingand, also, too, as well as, in addition, moreover (formal)
contrastbut, however, although, on the other hand, in spite of
reason / resultbecause, so, as, therefore, that is why
orderfirst, then, after that, finally, at the end
examplefor example, for instance, such as

Also use reference words so you do not repeat nouns: My brother has a new job. He likes it very much. This is good news.

4. Unit 4 examples

Informal email

Task: Your friend is looking for a part-time job. Write and give some advice.

Hi Dace,

Great to hear from you! I'm sorry you haven't found a job yet. Don't worry, you will find something soon. First of all, you should look at the website of the city job centre. Also, you could ask your friends: that's how I got my job in the café!

You don't have to write a long CV – one page is enough – but you must check it for mistakes. Finally, don't be nervous at the interview. You might get a surprise question, but if you stay calm, you'll do well.

Let me know how it goes!
Take care,
Liga

Formal email

Task: You saw a job advertisement. Write an email to apply for the job.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to apply for the position of sales assistant, which I saw on your website. I am 21 years old, and I am studying business at university. I have worked in a shop for one year, so I have experience with customers.

I can work part-time, and I should be available at weekends. I have attached my CV. Could you please tell me when the interviews will take place?

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
Liga Berzina

Notice: Both emails use modal verbs: you should look and you must check (advice and obligation), You don't have to (no need), I can work (ability). The formal email is structured clearly: reason for writing – experience – availability – ending.
