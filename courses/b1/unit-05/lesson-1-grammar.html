Unit 5 · Grammar: Comparing and describing: comparatives, superlatives, as ... as

Goal: compare people, places and things, and describe them in detail.

1. Comparatives (two things)

Type of adjectiveRuleExample
short (1 syllable)adjective + -er + thanRiga is bigger than Jūrmala.
ends in -y-y → -ierMy brother is funnier than me.
long (2+ syllables)more + adjective + thanTrains are more comfortable than buses.
irregulargood → better · bad → worse · far → furtherThis café is better than that one.

2. Superlatives (three or more things)

TypeRuleExample
shortthe + adjective + -estIt's the oldest building in the city.
ends in -ythe + -iestShe is the happiest person I know.
longthe most + adjectiveIt's the most beautiful park in Europe.
irregularthe best · the worst · the furthestThat's the best pizza ever!

After a superlative we often use in + place or of + group: the tallest building in Latvia, the best student of all. With the present perfect: the most interesting book I have ever read.

3. (not) as ... as

as + adjective + as = equal: Anna is as tall as Mara.
not as + adjective + as = less: Liepāja is not as big as Riga. (= Riga is bigger.)

4. Other ways to compare

  • a bit / slightly / a little + comparative (small difference): a bit colder
  • much / far / a lot + comparative (big difference): much more expensive
  • the same as / similar to / different from: My house is different from yours.
  • the + comparative, the + comparative: The more you practise, the better you get.
